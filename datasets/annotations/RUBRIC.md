# Answer-quality rubric (0-3)

Grade one answer to a general-knowledge or advice question. Read only the
question and the answer; do not compare against the paired answer.

- **3** - factually correct, directly answers the question, and names the
  mechanism or gives a concretely actionable step. Nothing misleading.
- **2** - correct and on-topic, but at least one of: a loose or debatable
  claim stated as fact, a filler sentence that adds nothing, or the "what"
  without the "why/how".
- **1** - partially correct or too vague to act on.
- **0** - wrong or off-topic.

## Files

- `items.jsonl` - 40 answers (20 pairs from
  `experiments/position_bias/results/2026-09-08.json`), regenerated by
  `tools/make_annotation_items.py`.
- `annotator_b.jsonl` - the repo author's grades, hand-assigned against this
  rubric. Not generated; treat as a fixed second annotator to compute
  agreement against.

## A property worth noticing before you compute kappa

The answers were deliberately generated to be "roughly equally good", so the
grade distribution here is compressed into 2s and 3s - no answer earned a 0
or a 1. On data like this percent agreement runs high while kappa stays
modest, because chance agreement on a two-grade-wide scale is already large.
That is not a flaw in the rubric; it is what a skewed label distribution does
to agreement statistics, and it is exactly why kappa is reported instead of
raw agreement.
